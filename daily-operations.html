Daily ETH operating budget

At 5 gwei effective gas price and an assumed 600,000 gas per range reset, the historical 2% anchor policy costs approximately 0.001676 ETH per day on average across all three currencies. Its observed busiest day costs 0.012 ETH. These are range-management fees under stated assumptions, not a measured all-in cost of a deployed protocol.

Gas assumptions and who pays

Operation Assumed gas Budget treatment
Fixed work for one currency reset, including report verification 150,000 Treasury/keeper
Remove, collect, convert inventory and reinstall each managed position 450,000 per position Treasury/keeper; gas only, no trading losses
Standalone authenticated report 100,000 Action sponsor; do not count twice inside a reset
Funded collateral/debt settlement 250,000 Protocol/backstop sensitivity; adapter-specific
User loan action including its ordinary report handling 400,000 User pays by default

None of these gas units is measured. No production contracts have been built. They are replaceable planning inputs; actual approvals, signature quorum, swaps, bridge proofs, calldata and adapter calls can materially change them. The gas RFP requires implementation and chain-specific measurements. Ethereum fees depend on gas used and the effective price, including priority fees; a transaction's gas limit or maximum fee is not its final cost. Ethereum gas documentation.

For n pooled positions per currency:

gas per reset = 150,000 + 450,000 * n
ETH/day = resets/day * gas per reset * effective_gwei * 1e-9

One shared managed position gives 600,000 gas per reset. Ten separately managed positions give 4,650,000 gas, or 7.75 times the reset fee. A pooled vault can account for many borrowers without moving an NFT for each borrower, subject to secure share accounting and withdrawal rules. An unbounded loop over borrower positions is unacceptable.

The 5% anchor row allows more than a 5% total-price deviation because the range already extends 3% below its anchor. The entire-band row is the closer sampled workload proxy for a 5% total-price objective. Neither guarantees continuous compliance.

At 5 gwei, ten positions per currency increase the 2% anchor mean to 0.012989 ETH/day and its historical peak to 0.093 ETH/day. Seed size of $10k versus $50k does not itself multiply gas if the position count and transaction flow remain equal. Small markets therefore bear a larger fee burden relative to their capital.

These are alternative/additive workload assumptions where operations are distinct, not observed traffic or forecasts. Batch reuse can reduce duplicated reports; failed calls and repeated attempts can increase costs. User-paid activity does not become treasury expense unless a paymaster or sponsorship rule makes it so. A pull-oracle design need not publish a transaction when no action requires it; frequent offchain monitoring still has infrastructure costs.

For illustration, 30 days of the 2% policy's historical mean plus two historical peak days is 30 * 0.00167598 + 2 * 0.012, approximately 0.0743 ETH, at 5 gwei and one position per market. This is a simple range-fee reserve example, not a sufficient liquidation reserve or congestion guarantee. Add funded stress settlements and the explicit sponsorship policy; price the same workloads again at adverse gas levels.

Current fee snapshot and its limits

An Ethereum mainnet eth_feeHistory response retrieved on 21 October 2026 at 11:31 UTC covered 1,024 blocks, numbers 26,239,667–26,240,690. Median base fee was 0.04454 gwei; the median of each block's base fee plus its gas-weighted 50th-percentile tip was 0.05929 gwei. These are a brief RPC sample, not a keeper inclusion quote or future fee forecast. A separate block-timestamp lookup was unavailable; the observation time is the retrieval time. The source response and calculation summary are retained in the private research repository. The 1/5/20-gwei tables deliberately remain scenarios rather than assuming this low fee period persists.

Costs outside the gas balance

Borrower ETH collateral, liquidation principal, exchange hedge margin, adverse selection, inventory conversion, slippage, bridge fees, L2 execution/data fees, data licenses, signing infrastructure, monitoring and security review are separate costs. Recording a pending liquidation does not eliminate these costs or the price exposure. Budget at least one permissionless funded path that works without new borrowers.

The proposed local-currency partnership scenario uses Zarban for IRR and separate matching stablecoin providers for TRY and ARS. It could reduce FX-driven range motion in all three relative pools, replacing it with each savings rate's drift and partner-peg events. Provider-specific rates, wrappers, any bridges and exit operations need their own workload study; the direct-FX reset counts above do not estimate that scenario.
