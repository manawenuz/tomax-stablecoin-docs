Liquidation and deferred settlement

The system may postpone external asset sales to conserve gas and avoid disorderly execution. Debt settlement, asset custody, and price exposure remain explicit during that interval. Marking a vault does not extinguish its debt.

State transitions

Active -> Flagged -> Funded takeover -> Inventory disposal -> Closed
                 -> Permissionless direct settlement -> Closed

Flagging uses accepted fresh collateral and FX data, locks risk-increasing owner actions, and records the reason and report sequence. Specify whether and how a borrower may cure the position, which debt continues accruing interest, and when ownership and surplus rights change. A flag cannot create collateral value, erase bad debt, or lock in an obsolete price for solvency reporting.

Funded takeover

A backstop supplies real debt tokens and burns the settled principal and accrued charges. It receives the collateral claim and bears subsequent price risk. For own-token LPs, recover the locked position and burn its own tokens first, then fund or source only the residual obligation through a reviewed atomic path. Avoid requiring keepers to source the entire gross debt if an internal netting path can safely reduce it.

The Liquity Stability Pool is a production precedent for burning debt and distributing collateral without requiring an immediate market sale. A proposed LP recovery or pooled-inventory extension remains new work.

Unfunded queue

If only flagged, the debt remains outstanding and collateral remains exposed. Revalue both continuously under accepted reports and reserve the resulting shortfall. Specify queue limits by value and age, funded bounties, priority, partial fills, dust, failed collateral recovery, and a maximum settlement deadline. Beyond the reviewed limit, restrict new exposure and activate a funded or orderly wind-down path.

An attacker must not be able to create enough dust flags to make settlement unbounded. Admission costs and keeper compensation must make small vaults economically executable.

Offchain hedging

A designated operator may hedge collateral already owned by the backstop. The design must state custody, execution venues, instruments, slippage, hedge latency, margin assets, leverage, funding costs, counterparty exposure, basis risk, settlement, and outage procedures. An ETH/USD short does not automatically hedge a debt denominated in IRR, TRY, or ARS.

An unexecuted order or an operator's reported hedge contributes zero collateral value. Onchain state must remain safe if the operator is offline, cannot open a short, is liquidated offchain, or loses venue access. Any proposal relying on unverified hedge claims as backing fails admission.

Borrower sponsored settlement

A new borrower may execute bounded queued work or pay an explicit capped execution contribution. Limit both gas and maximum obligation per call; permit permissionless settlement outside borrowing activity. Account for state changes and repeat the new loan's health and headroom checks after prior settlement.

Repayment and unambiguously risk-reducing collateral additions must not depend on clearing a queue. A system with no new borrowers must still be able to close its liquidation backlog. Paying gas in a later transaction defers its payer; gas savings require demonstrated batching or removal of redundant work.

Losses and surplus

The specification must assign borrower surplus, liquidation incentives, accrued interest, AMM fees, hedge gains and losses, custody losses, and backstop shortfalls. State the waterfall before accepting deposits. Queued collateral cannot simultaneously count as freely available quote liquidity, borrower collateral, and backstop inventory.

Design research. No production contracts or return promises. Public documentation; noindex is not access control.
